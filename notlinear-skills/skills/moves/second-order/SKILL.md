# Second-Order Thinking

Trace what happens after the immediate, obvious effect.

## How to execute

1. State the first-order effect of the choice (the obvious, intended outcome).
2. Ask "and then what?" — trace at least two more hops: who reacts, what incentive shifts, what gets crowded out, what becomes possible or impossible as a result.
3. Flag any second-order effect that could outweigh the first-order benefit.
4. Don't chase infinite hypotheticals — stop once the effects become too speculative to be useful, and say so.
