# Research: Academic

Primary literature grounding for claims that warrant a citation trail.

## How to execute

- Search by relevance and by citation count — a highly cited older paper is often more load-bearing than a recent uncited one.
- Read the abstract and method, not just the conclusion — the method tells you how much to trust the conclusion.
- Prefer recent survey/review papers when you need landscape context across many works, rather than reading primary papers one at a time.
- Distinguish a peer-reviewed, replicated finding from a single preprint — state which kind of claim you're citing.

## Output

Cite paper title, authors/year, and link. Note confidence — a single study is suggestive, a consistent literature is strong evidence.
